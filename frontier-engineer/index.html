The job description is the syllabus.

I'm working toward Lead Frontier Engineer, then Principal. This site turns my own company's job descriptions for those two roles into a study guide: the same areas of responsibility, in the same order, each broken into what I need to know, what I need to have built, and where to learn it for free. It presents my own restructuring of those role descriptions, not the job descriptions themselves.

In order, not in parallel

Two stages

Stage 1 · Parts 1–11

Becoming a Lead

Foundations, discovery and scoping, client co-creation, the technical delivery core, evaluation, security, change management, post-launch ownership, platform engineering, and advanced AI development. Ends in a readiness checklist.

Stage 2 · Parts 12–18

Lead to Principal

Transformation advisory, architecting for a squad, AI strategy, productization, thought leadership, and mentoring. Assumes Stage 1 rather than replacing it. Ends in a second checklist, plus the L1 to L2 delta.

What Principal adds to Lead

Scope

One engagement to many

Lead works one client under a Principal. Principal runs multiple clients or industries at once, working directly with CXOs.

Technical role

Build it, then architect it

Lead builds and productionizes the solution. Principal architects it and directs the squad building it.

Footprint

Client-facing to industry-facing

Lead's work is internal and client-facing. Principal also publishes, speaks, and builds relationships with vendors and academia.
