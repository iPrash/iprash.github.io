Independent review of a quote-to-cash landscape

Do eight short sessions, about 13 hours in total, over four or five days. The role is an independent architecture review of a CRM, CPQ, OMS and ERP chain that runs through MuleSoft. The client already has findings and a proposed fix. Your job is to test them against evidence and give a clear way forward. On the morning of the interview, read only the cheat sheet.

What this interview tests The panel wants to know if they can trust you with a critical, visible review. Expect five things, in this order of weight:
  • Assessment method. How you run a 4–8 week review: scope, evidence, findings, risks, options and a read-out. This carries the most weight.
  • Validating someone else's diagnosis. The team has a view and a proposed resolution. You test it. You do not start from a blank page.
  • Integration and performance risk. Chained synchronous calls, throttling, retries, duplicates, batch contention and weak observability across quote-to-cash.
  • Enough platform depth to be credible. The limits in Dataverse, Finance and Operations and MuleSoft that cause real symptoms. Feature lists matter less.
  • Client behaviour. Independence, bad news and a defensive team. The role is billable and the extension depends on the outcome, so expect a question on that.

Self-check: fifteen questions

Answer each one aloud in under 30 seconds before you open it. A miss sends you to the card named in the answer.

S0 · Triage

Where are the gaps?

1. What are the three Dataverse service protection limits?

Per user, per web server, over a 5-minute sliding window: 6,000 requests, 20 minutes of combined execution time, and 52 concurrent requests. Over the limit, the Web API returns 429 with a Retry-After header. Card Dataverse.

2. How long can a Dataverse plug-in run?

2 minutes. A synchronous plug-in in the pre-operation or post-operation stage also runs inside the database transaction, so a slow call there holds locks. Card Dataverse.

3. 400,000 sales order lines a night into Finance and Operations. OData or something else?

Something else. Microsoft's guidance is to use the batch data API (recurring integrations or the data management package API) above a few hundred thousand records. OData suits real-time, low-volume calls. Card Finance and Operations.

4. What happens when a dual-write transaction runs longer than 2 minutes?

It is rolled back on both sides. The timer covers Finance and Operations logic, the Dataverse write and any plug-ins on that table. Card dual-write.

5. Name the six parts of a quality-attribute scenario.

Source, stimulus, artefact, environment, response, response measure. Card ATAM.

6. Which two C4 levels do you draw for a current-state review?

System context and containers. Add one dynamic view of the quote-to-order flow, with volumes and sync or async marked on each arrow. Card C4.

7. Five systems in a synchronous chain, each 99.9% available. What is the chain's availability?

About 99.5%. Availability multiplies along a synchronous chain: 0.999 to the power 5. Latency adds. Card chained calls.

8. Three layers each try a failed call 3 times. How many calls reach the last system?

Up to 27 for one user action. That is a retry storm. Retry at one layer only, with backoff, jitter and a retry budget. Card retries.

9. How do you stop a retried "create order" from making two orders?

An idempotency key, such as quote number plus revision, checked by the receiver. In Dataverse use an alternate key with upsert. In MuleSoft use the Idempotent Message Validator with an object store. Card idempotency.

10. What does Unified Service Desk do, and what is its status?

A Windows desktop for contact-centre agents that hosts Dynamics pages and other apps in one shell. Deprecated from 1 Apr 2026. Microsoft points customers to the Copilot Service workspace. Card "USD".

11. What are the three layers of API-led connectivity?

System APIs unlock systems of record. Process APIs hold orchestration and business logic. Experience APIs shape data for one channel. Card MuleSoft.

12. A Mule flow returns 503 under load. What is a likely cause?

Back-pressure. The flow hit its maxConcurrency, or the thread pool is busy, and the HTTP listener rejects new events. Look at what is slow downstream before you add replicas. Card MuleSoft.

13. What four verdicts go in a findings validation matrix?

Agree, partially agree, disagree, insufficient evidence. Each verdict carries the evidence and a confidence level. Card matrix.

14. What is Little's law, and why does it matter here?

Concurrency equals throughput times latency. 20 orders a second at 3 seconds each means 60 requests in flight. If they all run as one integration user against one Dataverse web server, that passes the 52-concurrent limit. Card performance baseline.

15. What replaced Dynamics 365 release waves in 2026?

Continuous publishing to the AI at Work roadmap from Sep 2026. Release Planner retires by 15 Nov 2026. Card What changed.

A1 · Frame

Scope, charter and independence

A review fails most often in week 1, when the questions are vague and access is slow. Write a one-page charter and get the sponsor to sign it before you interview anyone.

The charter, one page

  • Questions to answer. Are the team's findings right? What risks and gaps did they miss? Will the proposed resolution work? What should happen next?
  • Scope. CRM, CPQ, OMS, ERP, MuleSoft, other middleware, named downstream systems. Out of scope: say it out loud, such as data migration or licensing.
  • Deliverables and dates. Interim view at week 2 or 3. Final report and read-out at the end.
  • Sponsor and steering group. Who receives the report. Who decides.

Independence terms

  • Read access to all artefacts, code, configuration and monitoring.
  • Access to all people, including vendors and the support team.
  • The delivery team can correct facts. It cannot edit conclusions.
  • You report to the sponsor. The team you review sees the draft to check facts.
  • Disagreements are recorded in the report, with both views.

Stakeholders to interview

Sponsor and business owner of quote-to-cash · project manager · lead solution architect · Dynamics 365 technical leads for Sales and for Finance and Operations · CPQ lead or vendor · OMS lead · MuleSoft platform owner and developers · infrastructure and security · support and operations (they know where it really breaks) · two or three sales and order-desk users.

Gotchas
  • Slow access eats a short review. Ask for the access list in the first meeting, with names against each item.
  • A scope without "out of scope" grows every week.
  • If the sponsor wants a predetermined answer, find out in week 1. Say how you will handle it.
Say this"Before I interview anyone, I agree a one-page charter with the sponsor: the questions we answer, the scope, the dates, and my independence terms. Read access to everything, access to everyone, and the team can correct my facts but not my conclusions."

A2 · Structure

ATAM, quality-attribute scenarios and the utility tree

ATAM is the SEI's method to evaluate an architecture against the qualities the business needs. A full ATAM takes several days of workshops. In a short review, run a light version: two workshops that build a utility tree and test the top scenarios against the architecture.

ATAM outputs

  • Risks: decisions that may cause a quality goal to fail.
  • Non-risks: decisions that are sound. Record them. They build trust with the team.
  • Sensitivity points: one setting that strongly drives one quality, such as the MuleSoft retry count.
  • Trade-off points: one setting that drives two qualities in opposite directions, such as sync dual-write (consistency up, availability down).

Light ATAM in a 6-week review

  1. Business drivers from the sponsor (week 1).
  2. Architecture walk-through by the team (week 1).
  3. Utility tree workshop: list scenarios, rank by business value and difficulty (week 2).
  4. Analyse the top 6–10 scenarios against the C4 views and the evidence (weeks 2–4).
  5. Present risks, non-risks, sensitivity and trade-off points (read-out).

ArtefactCRM quote, CPQ pricing service, MuleSoft process API

Gotchas
  • "The system must be fast" is not a scenario. Insist on the response measure. Without a number, no one can say a finding is right or wrong.
  • Do not sell a full ATAM to a client that needs an answer in six weeks. Name the method, then show the light version.

A3 · Current state

C4 views of the current state

The C4 model has four levels: system context, containers, components and code. For an assessment, draw the first two and one dynamic view. Draw them yourself from what you see in configuration and monitoring. Then compare with the team's diagrams. The differences are often the first findings.

What to add to each view

  • Context: every system, every user group, every external party. Mark which system owns which record: account, product, price, quote, order, invoice.
  • Containers: runtimes and data stores. Plug-ins, Power Automate flows, Mule apps, OMS services, Finance and Operations batch jobs, queues.
  • Dynamic view: a numbered sequence for quote save, quote submit and order create. Put latency and volume on each step.
Gotchas
  • Teams draw the design. Production runs something else. Build your view from configuration: plug-in registrations, flow triggers, Mule app list, scheduled jobs.
  • Unclear record ownership is a common root cause of duplicates and sync loops. Ask "who is the master for price?" early.
Say this"I draw the current state in C4: context and containers, plus one dynamic view of quote to order. I build it from configuration and telemetry, then compare with the team's diagram. Every arrow carries sync or async, volume, timeout and retry settings."

Review checklist by platform

  • Dataverse: synchronous plug-ins on quote, quote line, order. Filtering attributes set or not. Plug-ins that call external services. Flows triggered on high-volume tables. Async queue backlog.

A5 · Measure

Performance baseline and latency budget

You cannot validate a performance finding without a baseline. Pick five business transactions, measure them end to end at normal load and at peak, and split each one into a latency budget per hop.

The five transactions

  1. Quote save and price recalculation
  2. Quote submit for approval
  3. Quote to order (CRM to OMS)
  4. Order to ERP (OMS to Finance and Operations)
  5. Status and invoice back to CRM

For each: P50, P95 and P99 latency, throughput per minute, error rate, and the same at peak (month-end, quarter-end).

Tools for the math

  • Little's law: concurrency = throughput × latency. Use it to compare load with concurrency limits.
  • Utilisation: queues grow sharply above about 70–80% busy. A component at 90% at peak is a risk even if it works today.
  • Volumetrics: business drivers (quotes, lines per quote, orders) times calls per driver gives load per hop. This is your scale-modelling method from Story B.
Gotchas
  • Averages hide the problem. Users feel P95 and P99.
  • Test data with 5 quote lines will not show a problem that appears at 300 lines. Use the real distribution of quote sizes.
  • One shared integration user funnels all calls into one set of per-user limits. Check which identity each integration uses.
Say this"I baseline five business transactions end to end at P95, at normal load and at peak, and split each into a latency budget per hop using correlation IDs. Then I use Little's law and the volumetrics to check each hop against its limits. The biggest bar in the budget is where the fix goes."

A6 · Validate

Validate existing findings and the proposed fix

This is the core of the role. The team has findings and a proposed resolution. Test each one the same way, and record a verdict with evidence in the findings matrix.

For each finding

  1. Restate it as a testable claim. "The OMS is slow" becomes "order create in the OMS takes more than 4 s at P95 at peak".
  2. List the evidence the team used. Grade it.
  3. Reproduce or measure it yourself.
  4. List other explanations. Rule each one in or out.
  5. Give a verdict and a confidence level.

For the proposed resolution

  • Does it address the root cause, or a symptom?
  • What does it break or move? A fix in one hop often moves the queue to the next.
  • What does it cost, and how long until it shows results?
  • Is it reversible? Can you test it in a small slice first?
  • What would prove it worked? Agree the measure before the change.
A common patternThe proposed fix is "scale up MuleSoft and add retries". If the real limit is Dataverse service protection or Finance and Operations throttling, more Mule replicas send more calls into the same limit. You get more 429 errors and the same throughput. Check where the queue forms before you agree to scale anything.
Gotchas
  • Agree with what is right, early and in writing. It earns the trust you need when you disagree.
  • "Insufficient evidence" is a valid verdict. Say what evidence would settle it.
Say this"I turn each finding into a testable claim, check the evidence behind it, measure it myself and look for other explanations. Each gets a verdict: agree, partially agree, disagree or insufficient evidence. For the proposed fix, I ask whether it hits the root cause and where it moves the bottleneck."

A9 · Present

The executive read-out

Executives want three things: the verdict, the risk, and the decision they must make. Put all three on the first page. Put the evidence behind it.

One page, in this order

  1. Verdict on the proposed resolution, in one sentence. "The proposed fix addresses two of the four root causes. It will not hold at quarter-end without change X."
  2. Top five risks, with score and owner.
  3. Recommendations in three horizons, with cost range and time to benefit.
  4. Decisions needed today, and who makes them.
  5. What is working. Name it. It is true, and it makes the rest easier to hear.
Gotchas
  • No surprises. Pre-wire each key finding with its owner before the meeting.

B1 · Map

The quote-to-cash chain

Quote-to-cash runs from the first opportunity to cash in the bank. Each step has a system that owns the record. Most integration problems happen at the handoffs, and most performance problems happen where a user waits for the next system.

Questions to ask at every handoff

  • Who owns the record, and who may change it after the handoff?
  • Does the user wait for the next system (sync), or get a status later (async)?
  • What happens when the next system is down or slow?
  • How do we know a record arrived exactly once?
  • What are the daily and peak volumes, and the growth forecast?
Say this"I map quote-to-cash as a chain of handoffs and ask the same five questions at each one: owner, sync or async, failure behaviour, exactly-once, and volume. The answers usually point straight at the risks."

B2 · Pattern risk

Chained synchronous calls

A user clicks "submit". CRM calls CPQ, which calls MuleSoft, which calls the ERP for a credit check and the OMS for stock. The user waits for the slowest path. Latency adds up along the chain. Availability multiplies: five hops at 99.9% each give about 99.5%.

Symptoms

  • Timeouts at the user interface at peak.
  • A slow downstream system makes upstream systems slow too.
  • Plug-ins or flows that call external services inside a save.

Fixes

  • Keep only what the user must see now in the sync path.
  • Make the rest asynchronous with a status the user can see.
  • Cache reference data (price lists, products) close to the caller.
  • Set timeouts that shrink along the chain, so the caller gives up last.
Gotchas
  • A synchronous Dataverse plug-in that calls out runs inside the database transaction and holds locks while it waits. It also counts against the 2-minute limit.
  • If each hop has a 30-second timeout, the outer caller times out first and retries while the inner call still runs.
Say this"In a synchronous chain latency adds and availability multiplies. I keep only what the user must see in the sync path and move the rest to a queue with a visible status. Timeouts shrink along the chain, so the outer caller gives up last."

B3 · Landscape risk

Point-to-point sprawl, and too many layers

With direct links between systems, each new system adds links to many others. Eight systems can need up to 28 links. Each link has its own mapping, security and error handling. API-led connectivity reduces this by putting reusable system APIs in front of each system of record.

The opposite failure also exists. Some estates route every call through experience, process and system APIs even when one hop would do. Each layer adds a network call, a payload transform and a place to fail.

How to confirm

  • Count interfaces per system from the Mule app list and the CRM and ERP configuration.
  • Look for direct calls that bypass MuleSoft: Power Automate HTTP actions, plug-ins with URLs, custom services.
  • Count Mule hops per user transaction in the traces.

Fixes

  • One system API per system of record. Everything else calls it.
  • Collapse layers that add no logic for high-volume, latency-sensitive calls.
  • An interface catalogue with owner, volume, pattern and SLA.
Say this"I check for both extremes: point-to-point links that bypass the platform, and calls that pass through three Mule layers when one adds all the value. The test is simple: each layer must earn its latency."

B5 · Failure risk

Retry storms

A retry helps when a failure is brief. It hurts when the target is overloaded. If three layers each try a failed call three times, one user action can send 27 calls to the last system. The system that was slow is now down.

Symptoms

  • Error rates jump suddenly after a small slowdown.
  • 429 or 503 counts climb with traffic flat.
  • Duplicate records after an incident.

Fixes

  • Retry at one layer only, usually closest to the target.
  • Exponential backoff with jitter. Honour Retry-After.
  • A retry budget: no more than a fixed share of calls may be retries.
  • A circuit breaker that fails fast when the target is down.
  • Never retry a non-idempotent call without an idempotency key.
Gotchas
  • In MuleSoft, check Until Successful scopes, connector reconnection strategies and the caller's own retries. They stack.
  • Retries also cost money on usage-based MuleSoft plans and count against Power Platform request limits.
Say this"I count retries across all layers, because they multiply. One retry layer, exponential backoff with jitter, honour Retry-After, a circuit breaker, and no retries without an idempotency key."

B6 · Data risk

Missing idempotency and duplicate orders

Networks and queues deliver at least once. Exactly-once delivery across systems does not exist in practice. So every receiver that creates records must recognise a repeat and ignore it. That is an idempotent receiver.

Dataverse: plug-ins, flows and service protection

Dynamics 365 Sales and Customer Service run on Dataverse. Dataverse stores the tables (account, opportunity, quote, quote line, order) and runs custom logic as plug-ins and Power Automate flows. Most CRM performance problems come from logic that runs at the wrong time, or runs too often.

The event pipeline

  • Pre-validation: before the main operation. Good for cheap checks.
  • Pre-operation and post-operation, synchronous: inside the database transaction. The user waits. Locks are held.

  • An integration that runs as one application user shares one set of per-user limits.
  • Bulk messages cannot be called from inside a plug-in.

C3 · CRM to ERP

Dual-write and virtual entities

Dual-write keeps Finance and Operations and Dataverse in near real-time sync, in both directions. It is transactional: the write to the other side is part of the original transaction. Virtual entities show Finance and Operations data in Dataverse without copying it. Each read is a live query to the ERP.

Dual-write limits

LimitValue
Transaction time2 minutes, both directions. Covers ERP logic, Dataverse write and plug-ins. Longer transactions roll back on both sides
Records per transaction1,000 from Finance and Operations to Dataverse. Dataverse to Finance and Operations: a payload limit of about 117 MB
Legal entities250 per live-sync transaction; 40 for initial sync
Transaction countDataverse service protection limits one way, priority-based throttling the other

Trade-offs

  • Gain: consistent data, little custom code, Microsoft-supported maps.
  • Cost: tight coupling. A slow plug-in in CRM can roll back an ERP posting. Both systems must be up.
  • Virtual entities: no copy and no sync, but every CRM read becomes an ERP call. Fine for low-volume lookups; risky on busy forms and views.

Alternatives

  • MuleSoft or Azure integration with async messaging. Looser coupling, more build.
  • Business events from Finance and Operations into a queue.
  • A mix: dual-write for master data such as customers and products, async messaging for orders.
Gotchas
  • Dual-write and MuleSoft syncing the same table can loop or overwrite each other. Ask for one owner per table and direction.
  • A large Finance and Operations transaction must be split into commits of 1,000 records or fewer.
Say this"Dual-write gives consistency by coupling the two transactions, with a 2-minute limit that includes CRM plug-ins. That suits master data. For high-volume orders I prefer async messaging with idempotent receivers, so a slow CRM does not roll back an ERP posting."

C4 · Configure, price, quote

CPQ concepts and products

CPQ software lets sales build a valid product configuration, price it with the right discounts and approvals, and produce a quote document. In a Dynamics landscape the CPQ is usually a partner product embedded in the CRM, or a separate service called from it.

The three parts

  • Configure: product models, options, rules and constraints, and the bill of materials they produce.
  • Price: price lists, discounts, contract prices, margin checks, approval workflows.
  • Quote: document generation, e-signature, revisions, and the handoff to order.

Where it fits with Dynamics

  • Dynamics 365 Sales has native quotes, price lists, product families and bundles. That covers simple pricing.
  • Supply Chain Management has constraint-based product configuration on the ERP side.
  • Partner CPQ products for Dynamics include Experlogix, Configit, Tacton, Conga and Epicor CPQ [S]. Names only; check which one the client runs.

Common performance risks

  • One pricing call per quote line. Large quotes become hundreds of calls.
  • Full recalculation on every line change.
  • Synchronous calls to ERP for price, stock, tax or credit during quote edits.
  • Rule engines that slow sharply with model size.
  • Catalogue and price sync from ERP that runs during working hours.
  • Quote revisions that copy every line, so tables grow fast.
Say this"In CPQ I look at how pricing scales with quote size: calls per line, recalculation triggers, and synchronous calls to the ERP during editing. The fix is usually batch pricing per quote and cached price data. A faster server rarely helps."

C6 · Order management

OMS and order orchestration

An order management system takes a confirmed order, checks it, splits it into lines that can be fulfilled, picks the source for each line and tracks it to delivery. It is a long-running process. Steps take minutes or days, so the design must be asynchronous and resumable.

Core functions

  • Order capture and validation
  • Decomposition into fulfilment lines
  • Sourcing and available-to-promise
  • Orchestration of fulfilment, shipping and invoicing
  • Status, changes, cancellations and returns

Design questions

  • Is the OMS or the ERP the system of record for the order?
  • Is the order lifecycle an explicit state machine?
  • How is a failed step undone? (saga with compensation)

Common risks

  • Synchronous stock checks against the ERP on every order line.
  • Status sync storms: every state change pushed to CRM as a separate update.
  • Order create in the ERP as a bottleneck at peak.
  • No clear owner for order changes after submission, so CRM and ERP drift.

Products

Microsoft had Dynamics 365 Intelligent Order Management, built on Dataverse ver. Check its current status on the lifecycle pages before you mention it. Many landscapes use another vendor's OMS or order features in the ERP.

Say this"I treat the order as a long-running process with an explicit state machine and one system of record. Steps are asynchronous, failures compensate, and status flows back to CRM in batches."

Q9 · "How do you prevent duplicate or lost orders?"

What they testDelivery semantics and operability.
Model answer

Assessment walkthrough

A case to practise in 35 minutes on a blank page, out loud. It tests the method, the integration risks and the platform limits together.

Practice case

"Quotes time out and orders fail at month-end. The team wants to scale MuleSoft and add retries."

Assume: quotes of up to 300 lines; quote submit P95 is 12 seconds and sometimes times out; at month-end, orders reach the ERP hours late and a few are duplicated. The team's findings: "MuleSoft cannot handle the load" and "the ERP is slow". Proposed resolution: double the Mule replicas and raise retries from 3 to 5.

1–2 · Clarify and map
  • Ask: peak volumes and growth; which CPQ and OMS; which identity each integration uses; is there tracing today?
  • Draw the container view: CRM on Dataverse, CPQ, Mule layers, OMS, Finance and Operations, downstream. Mark sync and async on each arrow.
  • Draw the quote submit sequence and the month-end order flow.
3 · Measure
  • Latency budget for quote submit at P95, split by hop (see A5).
  • 429 and 503 counts per hop at month-end. Mule replica CPU and memory.
  • All scheduled jobs on one 24-hour chart for a month-end day.
  • Daily reconciliation counts: orders in CRM, OMS, ERP.
4 · Hypotheses to test
  • H1: quote submit time grows with lines because of a synchronous per-line plug-in or one pricing call per line.
  • H2: at month-end the limit is downstream (Dataverse service protection or ERP throttling), not Mule.
  • H3: retries stack across layers and turn a slowdown into a storm.
  • H4: order create is not idempotent, so retries make duplicates.
  • H5: invoicing and data imports compete with the order flow in the batch window.
5 · Test the proposed fix
  • More replicas: help only if Mule saturates. If H2 holds, they push more calls into the same limit and raise the 429 rate.
  • More retries: if H3 and H4 hold, five retries per layer make the storm and the duplicates worse.
  • Expected matrix: "MuleSoft cannot handle the load" gets disagree or insufficient evidence. "The ERP is slow" gets partially agree: slow under contention at month-end.
6 · Verdict and options
  • Verdict: "The proposed fix treats the symptom and is likely to make month-end worse. Two of the root causes are elsewhere."
  • Stabilise (30 days): one retry layer with backoff and Retry-After; spike control in front of Dataverse; throttling priority for the order integration; move reporting jobs out of the order window; correlation IDs and a DLQ alert.
  • Strengthen (90 days): batch pricing per quote; move per-line logic out of synchronous plug-ins; make quote-to-order asynchronous with an idempotency key; bulk messages for volume; load test at forecast peak.
  • Evolve: review whether dual-write or MuleSoft owns each table; plan any platform migrations from the What changed list.
GotchaDo not jump to the verdict. The panel scores the method. Say "I would expect to find" and "I would need to measure", never "the answer is".
