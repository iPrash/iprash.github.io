AWS Enterprise Architect · Interview prep

AWS deployment patterns, refreshed

Do seven sessions, about 12 hours in total. The site is built around the twelve deployment patterns that AWS architecture interviews ask about most. Each pattern card gives the shape, the services, the decisions, what changed in 2024–26, and free material to go deeper. On the morning of the interview, read only the cheat sheet.

What an AWS EA panel tests An enterprise architect panel spends less time on service features and more time on the layer above them. Be ready for four things:
  • The multi-account foundation. How you organise accounts, identity, guardrails and networks before the first workload lands. This is the most common EA question on AWS.
  • Trade-offs by pillar. Every answer names the Well-Architected pillar it serves and what it costs in another pillar.
  • Current vocabulary. Naming a retired service (CodeStar, App Mesh, QLDB, Migration Hub) tells the panel you have been away. The What changed section fixes this.
  • Programme thinking. Migration waves, a landing zone as a product, FinOps, and how a partner delivers this at scale.

S0 · Triage

Where are the gaps?

1. What is the difference between an SCP and an RCP?

An SCP limits what principals in your organisation can do. An RCP limits what can be done to your resources, by anyone, including principals outside the organisation. Together they build a data perimeter. Card 01.

2. You need 40 VPCs across 3 Regions to talk to each other and to on-premises. What do you use?

Transit Gateway per Region with peering, or Cloud WAN if you want one global policy and segments. Direct Connect to on-premises through a Direct Connect gateway. Card 02.

3. Name the four DR strategies, cheapest first.

Backup and restore, pilot light, warm standby, multi-site active/active. Card 08.

4. What replaced IAM users for human access?

IAM Identity Center with federation to the corporate IdP, and short-lived role credentials. IAM users stay only for rare machine cases that cannot use roles. Card core: network and identity.

5. When would you pick ECS over EKS?

When the team does not need the Kubernetes API or its ecosystem. ECS has less to operate and is deeply tied into AWS. Pick EKS for portability, a Kubernetes skill base, or tooling such as Argo CD and Karpenter. Card 05.

6. A team wants to start a new project on Migration Hub. What do you tell them?

Migration Hub closed to new customers on 7 Nov 2025. Use AWS Transform for assessment and planning, and Application Migration Service for rehosting. Card 09.

7. What are the 7 Rs?

Retire, retain, rehost, relocate, repurchase, replatform, refactor. Card 09.

8. What is the Lambda maximum run time, and what do you do when a workflow runs longer?

15 minutes. For longer work use Step Functions, or Lambda durable functions (Dec 2025), which checkpoint and can wait up to a year without paying for idle time. Card 04.

9. SQS, SNS, EventBridge, Kinesis, MSK: which one for "many consumers, replay, strict order per key"?

MSK (Kafka) or Kinesis Data Streams. Both keep order per partition or shard and allow replay. SQS and SNS do not replay. EventBridge has archive and replay, but no ordering guarantee. Card 06.

10. Name the six Well-Architected pillars.

Operational excellence, security, reliability, performance efficiency, cost optimisation, sustainability. Card Well-Architected.

11. What is AgentCore, and what does it replace?

Bedrock AgentCore (GA Oct 2025) is a managed set of services to run AI agents in production: runtime, memory, gateway, identity, observability, policy. It works with any framework and model. The older Bedrock Agents became "Agents Classic" and closed to new customers on 30 Jul 2026. Card 10.

12. A Region fails. What do you need to have in place already for a 15-minute RTO?

Warm standby or active/active in a second Region. Data replicated asynchronously (Aurora Global Database, DynamoDB global tables, S3 replication). A tested, scripted failover: ARC Region switch or Route 53 controls. Infrastructure as code for the second Region. Card 08.

13. What is the fastest way to cut the EC2 bill without changing code?

Rightsize, then commit with Savings Plans, then move suitable workloads to Graviton. Schedule non-production to stop at night. Card FinOps.

14. Where do you store vectors for RAG on AWS today?

Options: S3 Vectors (GA Dec 2025, lowest cost, large scale), OpenSearch Service, Aurora PostgreSQL with pgvector. Or let Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base (GA Jun 2026) run the whole pipeline. Card 10.

15. CDK, CloudFormation or Terraform for a 300-account estate?

All three work. Terraform if the estate is multi-cloud or the team already uses it. CDK if teams write code and stay on AWS. CloudFormation StackSets for org-wide baselines. Pick one per layer and write it down as an ADR. Card 11.

Core · 3 of 3

Network and identity basics

Network

  • Region → Availability Zones (separate data centres, low-latency links). Spread every tier across at least 2 AZs, 3 for quorum systems.
  • VPC: your private network in one Region. Subnets live in one AZ. Public subnets route to an internet gateway. Private subnets go out through a NAT gateway.
  • Security groups are stateful, per instance or interface. Network ACLs are stateless, per subnet. Security groups do most of the work.
  • VPC endpoints: gateway endpoints for S3 and DynamoDB (free), interface endpoints (PrivateLink) for other services. They keep traffic off the internet and cut NAT cost.
  • Route 53 for DNS, with health checks and routing policies: failover, latency, weighted, geolocation.
  • CloudFront for the edge, with WAF and Shield in front.

Identity

  • Humans: IAM Identity Center, federated to Entra ID or Okta, with permission sets mapped to roles in each account. No long-lived keys.
  • Workloads: IAM roles. EC2 instance profiles, ECS task roles, EKS Pod Identity, Lambda execution roles.
  • Policy evaluation: an explicit deny wins. Otherwise an action needs an allow, and nothing above it (SCP, RCP, permission boundary, session policy) can block it.
  • Cross-account: a role in the target account with a trust policy. Or resource policies, which RCPs can now limit.
  • Customer identity: Cognito user pools.
Gotchas
  • NAT gateway data charges are a common cost surprise. Add VPC endpoints for S3, DynamoDB and ECR first.
  • Overlapping CIDR ranges block peering and Transit Gateway routing later. Plan IP space centrally with VPC IPAM from day one.
  • Simple AD closed to new customers on 30 Jul 2026. Use AWS Managed Microsoft AD or AD Connector ver.
Say this"Identity is the new perimeter. People come in through Identity Center with short-lived credentials, workloads use roles, and org-level guardrails cap what any policy can grant."

Sources: Amazon VPC user guide; IAM policy evaluation logic; IAM Identity Center; AWS service availability, Jun 2026. Checked 29 Oct 2026.

01 · Foundation

Multi-account landing zone

A landing zone is the pre-built, governed multi-account environment that every workload lands in. Accounts are the strongest isolation boundary on AWS, for security, quotas and billing. An enterprise runs tens to thousands of them, grouped in organisational units (OUs), with guardrails set once at the top.

Building blocks

  • AWS Organizations: the account tree, consolidated billing, and policies.
  • Control Tower: sets up and governs the landing zone. Account Factory vends new accounts. It applies controls (preventive, detective, proactive).
  • Landing Zone Accelerator (LZA): an AWS solution that deploys a larger, compliance-aligned baseline on top of Control Tower from configuration files. Common in regulated industries.
  • Account Factory for Terraform (AFT): account vending through a Terraform pipeline.
  • IAM Identity Center: one sign-in, permission sets per OU or account.

Policy types

  • SCP: maximum permissions for IAM principals in member accounts. Example: deny leaving the org, deny unapproved Regions.
  • RCP ver: maximum permissions on resources (S3, KMS, STS, SQS, Secrets Manager and more). Example: no one outside the org reads our buckets. Nov 2024.
  • Declarative policies ver: set a service configuration across the org, such as "block public AMI sharing". Dec 2024.
  • Centralised root access ver: remove root credentials from member accounts and run privileged tasks from the management account. Nov 2024.
  • Tag policies, backup policies and AI opt-out policies.

Changed 2024–26

  • Control Tower landing zone 4.0 (17 Nov 2025) ver: a controls-only option. Config, CloudTrail, security roles and Backup integrations became optional, and a Security OU is no longer required. You can now adopt Control Tower governance on an existing estate without its full baseline.
  • Automatic account enrolment (Oct 2025): moving an account into an OU applies that OU's baselines and controls.
  • Fewer drift events (Aug 2025): SCPs attached outside the console no longer cause drift.
  • The Controls Library is now the Control Catalog, with mappings to frameworks such as PCI DSS 4.0.
Gotchas
  • SCPs do not affect the management account. Keep workloads out of it.
  • SCPs and RCPs never grant. They only cap. Teams still need IAM policies.
  • Test SCPs on a sandbox OU first. A bad deny at the root can lock out every account.
  • Treat the landing zone as a product with an owner, a backlog and versions. A one-off project decays.
Say this"I treat the account as the unit of isolation and the landing zone as a platform product. Control Tower or LZA gives the baseline, SCPs and RCPs together give a data perimeter, and account vending through a pipeline means a new team gets a compliant account in hours instead of weeks."

Go deeper (free): Organizing your AWS environment (whitepaper); AWS Security Reference Architecture; Control Tower workshop.

02 · Foundation

Network topology and hybrid connectivity

Enterprises run a central network account. It owns the hub, the links to on-premises, shared egress and inspection, and DNS. Workload VPCs attach as spokes. The choice is how the hub is built.

OptionUse whenNote
VPC peeringA few VPCs, high volume between two of themNot transitive. No extra charge per GB beyond normal data transfer. Does not scale to many VPCs.
Transit GatewayTens to thousands of VPCs in a Region, plus on-premisesRegional hub. Route tables segment traffic. Peer TGWs across Regions.
Cloud WANGlobal network across many Regions and sites, run as one policyCentral policy document defines segments. AWS builds and runs the TGW-like core for you.
PrivateLinkExpose one service to many consumers without joining the networksOne-way, by service. Overlapping CIDRs do not matter. Also how SaaS vendors connect.
VPC LatticeService-to-service traffic across accounts and VPCs, with auth policiesApplication-layer (HTTP, gRPC, TCP) network. Replaces mesh-style designs. App Mesh ended 30 Sep 2026 ver.
Site-to-Site VPNQuick start, or backup to Direct ConnectRuns over the internet. About 1.25 Gbps per tunnel.
Direct ConnectSteady, high-volume, low-jitter link to on-premisesDedicated or hosted links. Two locations for resilience. Use a Direct Connect gateway to reach many Regions.

Changed 2024–26

  • VPC encryption controls (Nov 2025) ver: audit, then enforce, that all traffic in a VPC is encrypted in transit. Declarative controls added Jul 2026.
  • VPC Block Public Access (Nov 2024) ver: one setting that blocks internet traffic to and from VPCs in a Region, with exclusions.
  • Route 53 Global Resolver (preview, Dec 2025): anycast DNS for public and private names across hybrid sites.
Gotchas
  • Transit Gateway charges per attachment hour and per GB processed. Very chatty VPC pairs can be cheaper on peering.
  • Central egress saves NAT gateways but adds a hop and a shared failure domain. Deploy it in every AZ.
  • Hybrid DNS is where projects stall. Plan Resolver inbound and outbound endpoints and the forwarding rules early.
Say this"Network is a shared platform in its own account. I segment by route table, inspect north-south and east-west traffic at one place, and use PrivateLink or Lattice when teams need one service and no wider network access."

03 · Application

Three-tier web application

The classic shape, and still the most common target for a replatform. Web, application and data tiers, each spread across AZs.

Users → Route 53 → CloudFront + WAF → ALB (public subnets, 3 AZs)
      → app tier: ECS/Fargate or EC2 Auto Scaling (private subnets)
      → ElastiCache → Aurora (writer + readers, Multi-AZ)
      Static assets → S3 (origin access control)

Key decisions

  • Session state out of the app tier, into ElastiCache or DynamoDB, so instances stay disposable.
  • Scaling signal: target tracking on request count per target or CPU. Scale the database separately with read replicas or Aurora Serverless v2.
  • Secrets in Secrets Manager with rotation. No credentials in images.
  • Deploys: blue/green or rolling with health checks behind the ALB.

Reliability levers

  • Multi-AZ everything. An AZ loss must cause no outage.
  • ALB health checks remove bad targets. Auto Scaling replaces them.
  • Aurora failover is usually under 30 seconds. RDS Proxy keeps connection pools stable through it.
  • Use timeouts, retries with backoff and jitter, and circuit breakers between tiers.
Gotchas
  • Connection storms from many app instances can exhaust the database. RDS Proxy fixes this.
  • Cross-AZ data transfer is charged. Chatty tiers in different AZs add up.
Say this"Stateless app tier, state in managed services, every tier across three AZs. That gives AZ-level resilience without a second Region. Multi-Region is a separate business decision about RTO and cost."

Go deeper (free): Reliability pillar; Amazon Builders' Library (timeouts, retries, backoff, static stability). Checked 29 Oct 2026.

04 · Application

Serverless API and workflows

Client → API Gateway (REST or HTTP API) or Lambda function URL
       → Lambda → DynamoDB
       Long or multi-step work → Step Functions, or Lambda durable functions
       Async fan-out → EventBridge / SQS → Lambda
       Auth → Cognito or a Lambda authorizer

Key decisions

  • HTTP API vs REST API: HTTP API is cheaper and faster. REST API adds usage plans, API keys, request validation and private APIs.
  • Orchestration: Step Functions for visual, auditable workflows with many service integrations. Lambda durable functions ver (Dec 2025) for workflows written as ordinary code, with checkpoints and waits of up to one year without idle cost.
  • Concurrency: reserved concurrency protects downstream systems. Provisioned concurrency removes cold starts.
  • Steady heavy load: Lambda Managed Instances ver runs the same functions on EC2 capacity at EC2 prices.

Gotchas

  • API Gateway has a 29-second default integration timeout. Long work goes async.
  • Lambda scales faster than most databases. Use SQS as a buffer, or RDS Proxy.
  • Retries happen. Make handlers idempotent (Powertools for AWS Lambda has an idempotency utility).
  • Observability needs structured logs, X-Ray or OpenTelemetry traces and metrics from the first day.
Say this"Serverless removes servers. I still design for idempotency, back-pressure and limits, and I watch unit cost, because at steady high load containers or Managed Instances can be cheaper."

06 · Integration

Event-driven and messaging

The question is always which service. Match on four things: fan-out, ordering, replay and throughput.

ServiceModelOrderReplayUse for
SQS StandardQueueBest effortNoDecoupling and buffering work. Nearly unlimited throughput.
SQS FIFOQueuePer message groupNoOrdered commands, exactly-once processing in a 5-minute dedup window.
SNSPub/sub pushFIFO topics onlyNoFan-out to queues, Lambda, HTTP, email, SMS.
EventBridgeEvent bus with rulesNoArchive and replayRouting domain events across accounts and SaaS. Schema registry. Pipes and Scheduler.
Kinesis Data StreamsStreamPer shardUp to 365 daysClickstream and telemetry, AWS-native.
MSK (Kafka)StreamPer partitionBy retentionKafka ecosystem, high throughput, existing Kafka skills. MSK Serverless and MSK Connect.
Amazon MQBrokerPer queueNoLift-and-shift of ActiveMQ or RabbitMQ apps.

Patterns

  • Fan-out: SNS → many SQS queues, or EventBridge rules.
  • Transactional outbox: write the event with the data, then publish. DynamoDB Streams or a CDC tool reads the outbox.
  • Saga: Step Functions for orchestration, events for choreography.
  • Stream processing: Managed Service for Apache Flink. Kinesis Data Analytics for SQL ended 27 Jan 2026.

Gotchas

  • Every option is at-least-once somewhere. Consumers must be idempotent.
  • Give every queue a dead-letter queue and an alarm on its depth.
  • EventBridge is not a stream. Do not use it where order or high replay volume matters.
Say this"Queues for work, topics or EventBridge for notifications, streams for ordered history. I choose by the consumer's needs. Between MSK and Kinesis, the trade-off is the Kafka ecosystem against operational simplicity."

Your Kafka work carries over directly: MSK is Kafka, so partitions, consumer groups and lag behave the same.

07 · Data

Data lake and lakehouse

Sources → ingest: DMS (CDC), Kinesis / Firehose, MSK, AppFlow, Glue
        → S3 raw → S3 curated (Iceberg tables, often S3 Tables)
        → catalog: Glue Data Catalog · governance: Lake Formation
        → query: Athena, Redshift, EMR, SageMaker Unified Studio
        → consume: QuickSight dashboards, ML, RAG

Key decisions

  • Open table format: Apache Iceberg is now the AWS default. S3 Tables ver (Dec 2024) manages compaction and snapshots for you, with replication and Intelligent-Tiering added Dec 2025.
  • Governance: Lake Formation for fine-grained access (row and column). Data mesh: producers own data products, a central catalog shares them.
  • Warehouse vs lake query: Redshift Serverless for BI concurrency. Athena for ad hoc SQL on S3.
  • Zero-ETL integrations from Aurora, RDS and DynamoDB into Redshift remove many pipelines.

Changed 2024–26

  • SageMaker Unified Studio brings data, analytics and AI work into one place.
  • S3 Select and Glacier Select closed to new customers (Jul 2024). Use Athena.
  • Lake Formation Governed Tables ended 31 Dec 2024. Use Iceberg.
  • S3 annotations (Jun 2026) ver: attach queryable context to objects for AI agents.
Say this"S3 and Iceberg as the single copy, many engines on top, and governance in Lake Formation. That avoids a separate copy per tool and keeps the data ready for AI use cases."

08 · Resilience

Multi-Region resilience and disaster recovery

Start from the business: RTO (how long down) and RPO (how much data lost) per application tier. The strategy follows from those two numbers. Multi-AZ handles most failures. Multi-Region handles Region-wide events and regulatory needs, and costs much more.

Building blocks

  • Data: Aurora Global Database, DynamoDB global tables, S3 Cross-Region Replication, AWS Backup cross-Region copy.
  • Traffic: Route 53 failover records, Global Accelerator.
  • Orchestration: ARC Region switch ver (Aug 2025) runs a tested, ordered failover plan. Aurora scaling and Lambda event-source blocks were added in 2026.
  • Servers: Elastic Disaster Recovery (DRS) for block-level replication of servers.
  • Testing: AWS Fault Injection Service for game days.

Design principles

  • Static stability: the standby works without calling the failed Region.
  • Use the data plane, not the control plane, during failover. Control planes are less available.
  • Cell-based architecture limits blast radius inside a Region.
  • A plan you have not rehearsed is a hope. Test failover on a schedule.
Gotchas
  • Active/active needs an answer for write conflicts. DynamoDB global tables use last-writer-wins. Aurora DSQL gives strong consistency across Regions at a latency cost.
  • Quotas, AMIs, secrets and KMS keys must already exist in the second Region.
  • The ARC Readiness Check feature closed to new customers in Apr 2026 ver.
Say this"I tier applications by RTO and RPO with the business, then fund the strategy per tier. Most get multi-AZ plus backup and restore. Only the few that earn it get warm standby or active/active, and every tier gets a tested runbook."

Security and observability baseline

Every account gets the same baseline on day one, set from the org level with delegated administrators in the security tooling account.

Security baseline

  • CloudTrail org trail to the log archive account. Immutable with S3 Object Lock.
  • Config recording in every account and Region, with conformance packs.
  • GuardDuty for threat detection. Extended Threat Detection finds multi-stage attacks, now across EC2 and ECS too ver.
  • Security Hub ver: the new version (GA Dec 2025) correlates signals and ranks risk in near real time. The older product is now Security Hub CSPM.
  • Inspector for vulnerability scanning of EC2, ECR and Lambda. Inspector Classic ended 20 May 2026.
  • Macie for sensitive data in S3. IAM Access Analyzer for external and unused access.
  • KMS keys, Secrets Manager, ACM. WAF and Shield at the edge. AWS WAF Classic ends 7 Oct 2026.

Observability baseline

  • CloudWatch: metrics, logs, alarms, dashboards. Cross-account observability into a central monitoring account.
  • Traces: X-Ray or OpenTelemetry (AWS Distro for OpenTelemetry). Application Signals for SLOs.
  • Managed Prometheus and Grafana for container estates.
  • CloudWatch unified data management (Dec 2025) normalises operations, security and compliance data with OCSF and Iceberg ver.
  • CloudTrail Lake closed to new customers on 31 May 2026. Query trails in S3 with Athena instead, or use CloudWatch.
Gotchas
  • Enable services in every Region, including ones you do not use. Attackers do use them. Or deny unused Regions with an SCP.
  • Findings without owners are noise. Route them by account tag to the owning team.
Say this"Detective controls are on in every account from the moment it is vended, centralised into a security account, with findings routed to owners. Preventive controls sit in SCPs and RCPs. Proactive controls sit in the pipeline."

Well-Architected Framework

AWS's shared language for trade-offs. Six pillars, each with design principles and review questions. A Well-Architected Review is a structured conversation with a team, run in the free Well-Architected Tool. It produces a list of high- and medium-risk issues and an improvement plan.

SecurityWho can do what, and would we know?Identity, guardrails, encryption, detection, incident response

Interview prep, question by question

Each question shows what the panel is testing and a model answer to adapt. Answer first in one sentence, then structure, then a number or an example. Two minutes per answer.

How to structure an AWS architecture answer

Requirements → Foundation → Pattern → Pillars → Operate

  • Requirements: users, volume, RTO/RPO, compliance, data residency, budget. Ask two clarifying questions before you draw.
  • Foundation: which accounts, which network, which identity. One sentence, because the landing zone already exists or comes first.
  • Pattern: name it (three-tier, serverless, event-driven) and draw it.
  • Pillars: walk two or three pillars and name the trade-off.
  • Operate: how it is deployed, observed, secured and paid for after go-live.
Fill before S6

Foundation

Q1 · "How would you set up AWS for a large enterprise starting from scratch?"

What they testWhether you start with governance and accounts, or jump straight to services.
Model answer

"I start with the landing zone, before any workload. Five parts:

  • Accounts: AWS Organizations with OUs for security, infrastructure, workloads split by prod and non-prod, and sandbox. One account per application per environment.
  • Identity: IAM Identity Center federated to the corporate IdP, permission sets per role, no IAM users for people.
  • Guardrails: Control Tower, or Landing Zone Accelerator in a regulated industry. SCPs to deny unapproved Regions and protect the baseline. RCPs for a data perimeter. Centralised root access.
  • Network: a network account with Transit Gateway or Cloud WAN, central inspection and egress, Direct Connect, IPAM.
  • Security and logging: org CloudTrail to a log archive account, Config, GuardDuty, Security Hub with a delegated admin.

Then account vending through a pipeline, so a team gets a compliant account in hours. I run the landing zone as a product with an owner and a backlog."

Foundation

Q2 · "How do you stop data leaving the organisation?"

What they testData perimeter thinking, and whether you know RCPs.
Model answer

"With a data perimeter on three conditions: only trusted identities, only trusted resources, only expected networks.

  • SCPs stop our principals from using resources outside the org (aws:ResourceOrgID).
  • RCPs stop principals outside the org from reaching our resources (aws:PrincipalOrgID), whatever a bucket policy says.
  • VPC endpoint policies and aws:SourceVpce conditions keep access on expected networks.
  • Plus Block Public Access for S3 and VPCs, Macie to find sensitive data, and KMS key policies.

Exceptions, such as a partner integration, are explicit and reviewed."

Network

Q3 · "Connect 200 VPCs and three data centres. Prod must never reach non-prod."

What they testTransit Gateway segmentation, hybrid DNS, scale limits.
Model answer

"A Transit Gateway per Region in the network account, shared through RAM. Separate TGW route tables for prod, non-prod and shared services. Prod and non-prod each propagate only to shared services and on-premises, never to each other. Direct Connect from two locations into a Direct Connect gateway, with VPN as backup. Route 53 Resolver endpoints for hybrid DNS. IPAM so no CIDRs overlap. If the estate spans many Regions, I would look at Cloud WAN, where the segments are one policy document instead of many route tables. For service-level access across segments, PrivateLink or VPC Lattice, so I do not open whole networks."

Security

Q4 · "A developer's access key leaks on GitHub. Walk me through it."

What they testIncident response, and whether your design prevents it.
Model answer

"Contain: deactivate the key, attach a deny-all policy to the user, revoke active sessions. Investigate: CloudTrail for every call with that key, GuardDuty findings, new resources or IAM changes. Recover: remove what the attacker created, rotate anything it could read. Then fix the cause: people should not have long-lived keys at all. Identity Center with short-lived credentials, OIDC for CI pipelines, an SCP that denies creating access keys except for an approved path, and secret scanning in the repository."

Application

Q5 · "Design a web application for 1 million users with spiky traffic."

What they testElasticity, caching, the database bottleneck.
Model answer

"Clarify read/write ratio and peak. Then: CloudFront in front, with static content from S3 and caching for read-heavy APIs. WAF on CloudFront. ALB to a stateless app tier on ECS Fargate across three AZs, scaling on request count. ElastiCache for sessions and hot reads. Aurora with read replicas, or DynamoDB if access patterns are simple key lookups. RDS Proxy to absorb connection spikes. Write spikes go through SQS so the database sees a steady rate. I would load test to find the next bottleneck, because moving one limit always exposes the next one."

Application

Q6 · "Serverless or containers for a new platform?"

What they testBalanced judgement, cost at scale, team fit.
Model answer

"It depends on three things: traffic shape, run time and team. Spiky, event-driven, short tasks: Lambda, because cost tracks use and there is nothing to patch. Steady high load or long-running services: containers on Fargate, or Lambda Managed Instances if the team wants to keep the Lambda model. Many teams on a shared platform with a Kubernetes skill base: EKS in Auto Mode. Most enterprises end up with both, so the platform team should give a paved road for each."

Application

Q7 · "Kubernetes on AWS: what would your platform look like?"

What they testPlatform engineering and multi-tenancy.
Model answer

"EKS in Auto Mode, a small number of clusters per environment, owned by a platform team. Namespaces per team with resource quotas, network policies and Pod Identity. GitOps with Argo CD. Images from ECR with scanning and signing, admission policies with Kyverno or OPA Gatekeeper. Observability through Managed Prometheus, Grafana and CloudWatch Container Insights. A version upgrade calendar, because EKS standard support is 14 months. Golden-path templates so a team gets a service with CI/CD, dashboards and alarms from one request."

Integration

Q8 · "SQS, SNS, EventBridge, Kinesis or MSK? Choose for an order platform."

What they testPrecise service selection, ordering and replay.
Model answer

"Order events per order must stay in order and be replayable for new consumers, so the order event stream is MSK or Kinesis, keyed by order ID. Domain events that other teams subscribe to, such as 'order shipped', go on EventBridge, because routing rules across accounts are simple there. Work queues behind each service are SQS with DLQs. Every consumer is idempotent, and I use the transactional outbox so the database write and the event cannot diverge."

Data

Q9 · "Build a modern data platform on AWS."

What they testLakehouse awareness, governance, AI readiness.
Model answer

"S3 as the storage layer with Apache Iceberg tables, using S3 Tables for managed maintenance. Ingest with DMS for CDC from databases, Firehose or MSK for streams. Glue Data Catalog and Lake Formation for governance, with domain teams owning data products in their own accounts. Athena for ad hoc, Redshift Serverless for BI, zero-ETL from operational databases where it fits. The same governed data feeds RAG through Bedrock Knowledge Bases, so AI does not create another copy."

Resilience

Q10 · "The business wants zero downtime. What do you say?"

What they testPushing back with numbers and options.
Model answer

"I turn 'zero' into RTO and RPO numbers per business capability, and show the cost of each step. Multi-AZ gives very high availability for a modest cost and covers most failures. Warm standby in a second Region gives an RTO of minutes for perhaps 1.3 to 2 times the cost. Active/active gives near-zero RTO but needs an answer for write conflicts and costs the most. I then fund the expensive tiers only for the capabilities that earn them, and test failover with ARC Region switch on a schedule."

Migration

Q11 · "Plan the migration of 800 applications from two data centres in 18 months."

What they testProgramme structure, tooling, risk.
Model answer

"Assess: discovery data and a portfolio assessment with AWS Transform, a business case, MAP funding. Mobilise in the first 3 to 4 months: landing zone, Direct Connect, a migration factory team, and a pilot wave of 10 to 20 low-risk applications. Then waves of 40 to 60 applications grouped by dependency. Most rehost with MGN or replatform databases with DMS. A smaller set gets refactored, with AWS Transform to speed up code work. Retire 10 to 20 percent from the discovery data. I track velocity per wave and adjust. The data centre exit date drives the plan, so the riskiest dependencies go early."

AI

Q12 · "How would you build an enterprise agent platform on AWS?"

What they testCurrent GenAI services and governance. Your strongest area.
Model answer

"Bedrock for models, with routing by task and cost. AgentCore for the runtime: isolated sessions, Memory for short- and long-term context, Gateway to expose enterprise APIs as MCP tools, Identity for delegated user access, Observability for traces. Bedrock Managed Knowledge Base or our own pipeline with S3 Vectors for retrieval. Guardrails for content and PII. An evaluation harness in the release pipeline. Human approval for actions that change money or customer data. One account per agent domain in the landing zone, with SCPs to keep model access to approved models and Regions. I built this shape before: [your platform, in two sentences]."

Cost

Q13 · "Our AWS bill grew 40% this year. Where do you look?"

What they testFinOps practice and a method. A list of tips scores low.
Model answer

"First, is it growth or waste? I check unit cost: if cost per transaction is flat, the business grew. Then the top five services and accounts in Cost Explorer, and the anomaly history. Usual findings: idle non-prod, oversized instances, missing Savings Plans coverage, NAT and cross-AZ data transfer, old snapshots and logs without lifecycle rules. Quick wins in weeks, then a FinOps operating model so it does not return: account-level ownership, budgets, a monthly review with engineering."

Partner access

Partner and internal portals

As an employee of an AWS partner, you can usually get more than the free tier:

  • AWS Partner Central: sign in with your company email. It links to partner training, the partner learning plans in Skill Builder, and accreditations such as "AWS Partner: Technical Accredited". Partner Central is now also reachable from the AWS console (Dec 2025).
  • Partner Skill Builder access often includes labs and AWS Jam events, which give you real AWS accounts for the lab session.
  • Your company's AWS practice: ask for its reference landing zone, migration factory method, and a recent anonymised case study. These give you the "how we deliver" language that a partner's panel expects.

Access depends on your company's partner tier and programme enrolment. Confirm in Partner Central.
